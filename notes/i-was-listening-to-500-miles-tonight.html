I Was Listening to '500 Miles' Tonight

Belonging HW · 08.08.2026

For the better part of a decade, I have been moving through homes, friends, and communities. Some of it was outside my control. Some of it was entirely within it, because I chose the life that required me to keep leaving.

Leaving people you love behind is always hard. You settle somewhere new. You find your people. You learn the rhythms of a place. Eventually, the unfamiliar becomes ordinary. And then, sometimes, you leave again.

There is always nostalgia.

Looking back across a decade, I realize something stranger: I am no longer the person who began this journey. Somewhere among all the places and people I left behind, I left versions of myself behind too.

I miss people, places, circumstances that no longer exist. Sometimes I even miss the person I used to be.

But there is a peculiar happiness inside that sadness. A kind of freedom that is difficult to explain.

I am no longer bound by everything I hold dear.

Perhaps because of that, I have become less afraid of unfamiliar rooms. I can sit at tables full of strangers, listen, and make genuine connections with people from all walks of life. I don't need everyone to think like me or sort people into tribes of us and them.

I can simply meet another human being and see who they are.

Maybe that is one of the unexpected gifts of leaving so many times.

Homes disappear. Communities change. Friendships stretch across oceans and sometimes quietly end. The person you once were becomes someone you remember with affection but can never quite return to.

Eventually, you understand that perhaps you do not completely belong anywhere—or to anyone.

Perhaps freedom lives somewhere beyond nostalgia: not in forgetting what we loved, but in realizing we can carry it without needing to return to it.

Tonight, I can sit alone on my couch, hear an old folk song about being far from home, feel the weight of every place and person I have left behind—and still know that I am okay.

Maybe even happy.

Maybe home, after all, is not the place I am trying to get back to.

Maybe it is the person who kept walking.
