Is Being Funny a Trait?

Belonging HW · 07.25.2026

It's funny how sometimes the people around us remember parts of ourselves that we've forgotten.

I've never really thought of myself as someone with a comic personality. Yet throughout my life, friends have often told me that one of my defining traits is my ability to make conversations unexpectedly funny.

A friend reminded me of this recently. She said that, regardless of everything that has changed over the years, one thing has remained constant: my knack for making people laugh—not through rehearsed jokes, but through the odd observations, strange analogies, and little anecdotes that naturally find their way into conversation.

The truth is, most of my jokes aren't even intentional. I simply say what I'm thinking, exactly as I see it. Sometimes it's the unusual perspective. Sometimes it's the complete seriousness with which I describe something utterly ridiculous. Half the time, I don't realize it's funny until everyone else starts laughing—and then I start laughing too.

I don't think humor is something I perform. I think it's simply the lens through which I experience the world.

There was a time, though, when that lens became cloudy; beneath the weight of deadlines, uncertainty, and the constant pressure to prove myself, I slowly lost that spark. I became so focused on surviving that I forgot there was a version of me who delighted in finding the absurdity hidden in ordinary moments.

Recently, I've begun to notice her returning.

Perhaps she never really left.

What moves me most is realizing that my closest friends never forgot that version of me, even when I couldn't see her myself. They carried that memory for me until I was ready to rediscover it.

There is something deeply comforting about being known that well.

Sometimes the people who love us don't just walk beside us through life—they quietly hold onto pieces of who we are until we're able to find our way back to them.

For that, I am profoundly grateful.
