On Human Information Transfer

Belonging HW · 06.26.2026 · Entry 1

Recently I found myself thinking about communication—not just what we say, but how conversations shape our relationships.

In science, we often talk about information transfer: how information moves from one system to another. Human conversations feel similar. We have thoughts, emotions, and experiences inside us, and we try to translate them into words another person can understand.

So what is efficiency in human information transfer?

I don't think good communication means filtering every thought before we speak. With people we trust, we should be able to be spontaneous, imperfect, and ourselves. But there is a balance that allows communication to feel meaningful and energizing for both sides.

Some people naturally create space for others. They listen, ask questions, and make conversations feel mutual. With them, connection feels easy. But even in those relationships, it is important to remember that they also have stories and experiences they want to share.

With other relationships, the balance may be harder to find. Conversations can become one-sided, and over time that can leave one person feeling disconnected or drained.

I think emotional intelligence is partly noticing these patterns—not only how we express ourselves, but how our communication affects the person on the other side.

Ultimately, communication is a learning process. The balance is different with every person.

The goal is not to say less.

The goal is to create conversations where both people feel heard.
