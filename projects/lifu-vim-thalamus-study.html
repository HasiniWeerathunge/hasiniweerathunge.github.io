Noninvasive Thalamocortical Neuromodulation With Low-Intensity Focused Ultrasound for Persistent Developmental Stuttering

NCT07668882 IRB-Approved Human Study

This study investigates low-intensity focused ultrasound (LIFU) as a noninvasive method for modulating the ventral intermediate (VIM) thalamus, a key node in circuits governing speech motor timing and coordination. We are recruiting adults who stutter and neurotypical adult controls to characterize how targeted thalamic perturbation propagates through distributed cortical and subcortical networks, and how those network-level changes relate to speech motor behavior.

The study combines LIFU delivery with multimodal neuroimaging to link a causal circuit intervention to measurable changes in brain network activity and behavior — part of a broader effort to build individualized, image-guided neuromodulation approaches for speech and motor disorders.
